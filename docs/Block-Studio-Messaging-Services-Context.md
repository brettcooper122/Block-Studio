# Block Studio — Messaging & Services Context

Reference document for any agent (Claude Code or otherwise) generating copy, pages, or positioning for Block Studio. Written so the messaging and service definitions do not need to be re-explained in a fresh session.

## Brand voice rules

Apply these to any Block Studio-facing copy without being asked again:

- First person plural. "We" and "Block Studio," never "I."
- Canadian/British spelling: -our endings (colour, behaviour, favour), -ize verb endings (organize, recognize, realize).
- Confident, plain, specific. Short declarative sentences. Minimal self-praise adjectives ("beautiful," "stunning," "innovative"). Confidence comes from naming the buyer's world precisely, not from describing the work as impressive.
- Never use: em dashes, the word "genuinely," manufactured staccato (rapid-fire short sentences stacked for artificial punch), colon-lead sentences ("Here's the thing:"), or "that's not X, that's Y" contrastive constructions.
- Draw from the keyword bank below rather than generic design-agency language ("streamline," "innovative," "cutting-edge").

## Who Block Studio serves

**Founders, pre-seed to Series A**
- Shipping fast, but hiding weak signal behind velocity
- Cannot afford a full design hire, cannot risk a bad one
- Building for investors who want to see something real, not a Figma export
- Sitting on features that shipped but nobody adopts

**Small businesses and agencies**
- Design system exists but keeps drifting from what the product actually ships
- Chasing overflow work with no bench to catch it
- Guessing at what users want without the resources to run proper research
- Repositioning around AI without a designer who understands the shift

## Positioning formula

> For **[specific buyer]** who **[specific pain]**, we **[outcome in their words]**, by **[unfair advantage]**, not **[the old, slow way]**.

## The three services

### Service 01 — Agentic End-to-End UX Design & Development

**Pain it resolves.** Product teams building AI-adjacent experiences are stuck in a handoff loop built for a slower era. A designer ships Figma, engineering waits on tickets, and users get something four sprints later that half-answers the original brief. Once the product involves an AI agent, the loop compounds; static mocks cannot capture behaviour that is dynamic, probabilistic, and shifting by the day.

**What clients get.** Design and build, in the same set of hands, from the first sketch through to production-ready code. States, confidence displays, and handoff patterns for when the agent needs to pass control back to a person, all built in real code so the experience can be tested before the team commits to it.

**Approved copy:**
> "Most designers hand off files and wait weeks for engineering to bring them to life. We bring designs to life ourselves, in real code, so the product you test with users is the product your team can ship."

**Alternative hook (tighter, closer to LinkedIn voice):**
> "Static mocks cannot capture AI behaviour. Agentic UX has to be built to be designed; anything less is a guess dressed up in Figma."

### Service 02 — Creative Strategy: user & market research

**Pain it resolves.** Founders fall in love with a solution before confirming enough people have the problem, and have it badly enough to pay to solve it. The validation itself often carries a built-in trap: leading questions asked of socially connected respondents, feedback that confirms what the team already wants to hear. The result is a product built on flattering data, launched into a market that never asked for it.

**What clients get.** User interviews scoped to the exact question the business needs answered, competitive analysis that filters out the fluff and surfaces the patterns worth borrowing, and positioning work that connects the product to the language the market is already using in searches, forum posts, and reviews. Research that shapes the decision, not research that decorates one already made.

**Approved copy:**
> "Most teams design for the customer they imagine, then wonder why the real one never shows up. Creative strategy connects your product to the language the market is already using: the words your buyer types into a search bar, not the words your team writes on a roadmap."

**Alternative hook:**
> "Research should shape the decision, not decorate one already made. From the outset, this is about finding the pattern the market is showing you, so you build the product it is asking for."

### Service 03 — Rapid Pattern Generation & System Scaling

**Pain it resolves.** A design system exists, and it worked well for the first eighteen months. Now the product has grown past what the system was scoped for; new patterns are needed weekly, and the team is either rebuilding the system from scratch or watching hardcoded values quietly slip into production. Neither is sustainable. Neither is necessary.

**What clients get.** New components and patterns generated to slot into the existing system, respecting token hierarchy from primitive to semantic to component. Governance stays intact; the library keeps its integrity as it scales. This is the work agents cannot do alone, and juniors take too long to do carefully. It sits in the middle, and that middle is where the value is.

**Approved copy:**
> "Your design system does not need a rebuild. It needs someone who can extend it at the pace your product is actually shipping, without breaking the hierarchy it was built on."

**Alternative hook:**
> "Systems drift when growth outpaces governance. New patterns generated in the shape of your existing tokens, carrying the same rules, so scaling does not cost you the consistency the system was built for in the first place."

### How the three connect

Creative Strategy answers what to build. Agentic End-to-End brings the answer to life in real code. Rapid Pattern Generation keeps the system holding as the product scales past its original brief. Together they cover the arc from the question to the shipped feature, without the handoff gaps that eat the weeks of runway a founder does not have.

## What stays internal, not a public service line

Brett also named **automations** (built through Claude Cowork) as a fourth capability. This is internal delivery language, not a client-facing service. Do not create a fourth service page, nav item, or named offering for it. Where relevant, it can appear as an implementation detail inside Service 01 or Service 03 (for example, describing how a recurring audit or handoff gets kept up to date), but it is never named "Claude Cowork" or "automations" in client-facing copy.

## Keyword bank (draw from these, do not default to generic agency language)

adoption, conversion, retention, trust, activation, ship / shipped, end-to-end, production-ready, design system, rapid prototyping, validate, zero-to-one, explainability, agentic, investor-grade, sprint pace, complex-to-clear, business outcome

## Industries to name

**Strongest claim, lead with these:** enterprise/B2B product (Mitsubishi, Hyundai, Kraft Heinz), AI-native products (the working method itself is the credential), design systems (OpenText/Blueprint work).

**Credible with framing, once a case study exists:** fintech, loyalty/rewards (Scene+), marketplaces (Kijiji), automotive (Hyundai, Mitsubishi), SaaS/B2B tools, accessibility-critical products.

Lead with one or two rather than naming all of them at once.

## Site structure guidance

**Build:** one sharp hero line (pain plus outcome), the three named services above described by outcome, two to three case studies with a before/after and one real number, a short "who this is for and who it isn't" section (not yet drafted for Block Studio specifically), one clear call to action, and a simple About.

**Skip at this stage:** dozens of programmatic SEO service pages, subscription pricing tiers, a weekly blog commitment, awards walls, multi-office listings, heavy motion work.
